APPENDIX B — LIKELY QUESTIONS, SHORT ANSWERS
=====================================================================
Q: Why multiply the four scores instead of adding them?
A: Security must be conservative. Each channel expresses independent
   doubt; multiplying safety probabilities means doubt anywhere lowers
   the final safety — and any single channel at 70+ forces a dangerous
   verdict regardless of the others.

Q: How do you know the zero false-positive claim is real?
A: scripts/eval_corpus.py runs every engine against a labelled corpus
   — 22 phishing, 50 benign — and is wired as a pass/fail gate: today
   it returns PASS with 0 FP and 0 FN. The 428-test suite covers the
   rest.

Q: Is the scoring transparent or a black box?
A: Fully explainable — every engine card shows its score and
   contributing flags; header rules show weights; the report exports
   the complete breakdown plus integrity hashes.

Q: You are fetching a real university mailbox — what about privacy?
A: Attachments and raw evidence are stored hashed with an audit trail;
   PII masking and 90-day retention are implemented (configurable);
   credentials live only in server-side environment variables.

Q: What would you do next?
A: Dynamic URL analysis with headless-browser screenshots (plumbing
   already present, currently disabled), a four-class BEC retrain,
   offline MaxMind geolocation, larger labelled corpus, alerting
   integrations (email/Slack).

Q: Why was last night needed at all — wasn't it done earlier?
A: The functional work was complete; last night was an integration
   pass: five reviewer-style UX/pipeline defects found by using the
   product end-to-end, each fixed, tested (suite rerun to 428/78.19%),
   and redeployed to production the same night.
